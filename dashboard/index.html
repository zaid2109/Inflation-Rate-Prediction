What is inflation?
Inflation means prices are going up over time. If inflation is 3%, something that cost $100 last year now costs $103. A little inflation is normal and healthy — the Federal Reserve aims for 2% per year.
Why does it matter to me?
High inflation means your money buys less. Groceries, rent, and gas all cost more. It also affects interest rates on your mortgage, car loan, and savings account.
What is the Fed's 2% target?
The Federal Reserve (America's central bank) tries to keep inflation near 2% per year. Too low can slow the economy; too high erodes purchasing power. They raise or lower interest rates to steer inflation toward that target.
How is this forecast made?
We use machine-learning models trained on 35 years of US economic data — oil prices, unemployment, interest rates, money supply and more. The model finds patterns to estimate where inflation is headed.
